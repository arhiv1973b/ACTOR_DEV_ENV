## 1. Core Antagonism
- **Venice Commission Narrative ("Law as servant of democracy"):** A political-administrative construct created post-1990 by Council of Europe organs to introduce procedural filters, admissibility criteria, and political discretion, effectively subordinating human rights imperatives to bureaucratic consensus.
- **Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (1969, Articles 53 & 64):** Establishes that *jus cogens* (peremptory norms of general international law, such as the absolute prohibition of torture under UDHR 1948) renders void any treaty or state practice in conflict with it, and automatically supersedes inferior regional filtering mechanisms.

---

## 2. Institutional Subversion in Moldova & Romania
1. **Constitutional Supremacy:** National constitutions explicitly mandate the direct application and priority of international human rights standards (UDHR 1948).
2. **Administrative Override:** The Venice Commission's narrative was utilized by domestic elites and regional judicial bodies to construct procedural roadblocks (e.g., rigid statutes of limitations, exhaustion of local remedies doctrine) that shield state actors and institutional corruption (such as the 1997-2026 systemic cover-up and FinComBank financial blockade) from direct *jus cogens* enforcement.
3. **The Resulting Defect:** Subordinating the absolute imperative of UDHR Art. 5 to regional procedural rules created an artificial legal defect, which our evidence graph explicitly invalidates through the doctrine of *fructus venenati* and *erga omnes* obligations.

---

## 3. Conclusion
The administrative narrative of the Venice Commission cannot derogate from the absolute peremptory norms codified in the Vienna Convention and grounded in the UDHR. All domestic and regional filtering acts derived from this subordinate doctrine are null and void *ab initio*.
