## III. LEGAL GROUNDS FOR VIOLATION OF INTERNATIONAL LAW

**GROUND 1:** Systematic Evasion of Physical Verification (Hague Convention Art. 3 & UDHR Art. 8)  
Competent authorities are obligated to verify the physical original and the genuine capacity of the signatory. Labeling a signature “semnătura indescifrabilă” while issuing an automated electronic stamp constitutes unlawful abdication of verification duty.

**GROUND 2:** Decoupling of Object and Transaction (Object Substitution)  
An apostille must certify a specific, verifiable public document. Moldovan e-apostilles certify only an internal database transaction identifier, creating a legal fiction.

**GROUND 3:** Negligence and Cursory Authentication of Torture/Fabrication Records  
State-sanctioned certification of judicial acts involving torture and property expropriation without substantive review violates non-derogable peremptory standards (Jus Cogens).

**GROUND 4:** Misuse of Digital Signatures as Procedural Camouflage  
Cryptographic digital signatures are misused to authenticate the integrity of electronic file containers while concealing the substantive nullity of the underlying acts.

**GROUND 5:** Legal Nullity (Res Nulla) of Defective Apostilles  
The fundamental disjunction between the physical original and the electronic record renders the apostille void *ab initio*.

**GROUND 6:** Obstruction of Effective Remedy and International Rehabilitation (UDHR Art. 8)  
Defective apostilles effectively bar victims from introducing authentic evidentiary records into international tribunals, perpetuating institutional impunity.
