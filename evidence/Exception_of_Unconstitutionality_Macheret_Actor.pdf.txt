I. Subject-Matter of the Exception of Unconstitutionality
The Applicant challenges the provisions of the State Fee Law No. 213/2023 (as
subsequently amended) and the corresponding provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure
of the Republic of Moldova governing the stamp duty (taxa de timbru) and the conditions
for exemption therefrom, to the extent that they permit a court:
• to refuse exemption from, deferral of, or payment by instalments of the stamp duty in
respect of persons with Group I disability and victims of torture;
• to order the return of a statement of claim solely on the ground of non-payment of the
stamp duty, notwithstanding evidence of vulnerability, linguistic and cognitive barriers,
and the status of victim of torture;
• thereby to erect insurmountable procedural barriers incompatible with Articles 4, 20
and 53 of the Constitution of the Republic of Moldova and with peremptory norms of
international law (jus cogens).
II. Admissibility of the Exception
Pursuant to Article 12¹ of the Code of Civil Procedure of the Republic of Moldova and the
Judgment of the Constitutional Court of the Republic of Moldova No. 2 of 9 February
2016, the present exception satisfies the four cumulative conditions of admissibility:
1. The object of review falls within the category of acts listed in Article 135(1)(a) of the
Constitution of the Republic of Moldova.
2. The exception is raised by a party to the proceedings (Macheret Alexei / A©tor).
Page 2
3. The contested provisions were directly applied by both the first-instance court and
the Court of Appeal when deciding the request for exemption from stamp duty and the
return of the statement of claim.
4. No prior judgment of the Constitutional Court exists on the identical subject-matter
insofar as it concerns the application of these provisions to victims of torture and
persons with Group I disability in the context of systemic falsification of apostilles.
III. Relevant Facts
Judge Maria Muruianu refused the Applicant’s request for exemption from the stamp
duty and ordered the return of the statement of claim seeking recovery of damages
amounting to EUR 506,880. The Panel of the Court of Appeal Centru (Judges Ana Panov,
Svetlana Vișcu and Elena Bolocan) upheld that decision.
The Applicant is a person with lifelong Group I disability and a recognised victim of
torture. Despite this status, the courts imposed a fiscal requirement that effectively
barred access to justice.
In addition, the case file contains evidence of systemic falsification of apostilles. Of the

https://arhiv1973b.github.io/apostille-archive-english/, eleven apostilles contain, in the

handwritten signature of the issuing official. This distortion constitutes a direct breach of
the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961 Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for
Foreign Public Documents and deprives the affected documents of evidentiary value
essential to establishing the facts of torture and securing rehabilitation.
IV. Legal Argument
The Applicant submits that the ordinary courts’ approach — exemplified by the rulings
of Judge M. Muruianu and of the Panel of the Court of Appeal Centru composed of Judges
A. Panov, S. Vișcu and E. Bolocan — subordinates the protection of fundamental human
rights to fiscal requirements of a prohibitively high character, while simultaneously
disregarding the falsification of legalisation documents. This approach stands in
irreconcilable contradiction with Article 4 of the Constitution of the Republic of Moldova,
the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948), and peremptory norms of
international law (jus cogens).
Article 4 of the Constitution establishes the absolute priority of international
human-rights instruments. That priority cannot be reduced to a formal or uncritical
reference to the European Convention on Human Rights in its post-war formulation. The
European Convention of 1950 and the related mechanisms concerning state immunity
were, in substantial measure, instruments of legal stabilisation in Western Europe
following the Second World War. They introduced procedural limitations, limitation
periods and jurisdictional immunities that narrowed the absolute standards proclaimed
by the Universal Declaration of Human Rights of 1948. Such limitations cannot abrogate
the natural right to judicial protection and to full rehabilitation of a victim of torture.
The practice of leading European States (Germany, Italy and Greece) in disputes
concerning war reparations and jurisdictional immunities confirms that the national
constitutional order and peremptory norms protecting human rights cannot be
subordinated to administrative or conventional bureaucracy that creates the appearance
of justice while effectively denying access to court and concealing the systemic forgery of
official documents. Germany, in particular, declines in practice to accord full confidence
to apostilles issued in certain Eastern European jurisdictions and requires consular
legalisation where indications of technical distortion exist — precisely the situation
presented by the eleven apostilles in the Applicant’s public archive.
Page 3
In these circumstances, an automatic invocation of the “priority of the ECHR” risks
functioning as a device that protects the bureaucratic apparatus at the expense of the
victim. Article 4 of the Constitution of the Republic of Moldova requires the courts to
apply directly the absolute standards of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and
the norms of jus cogens (in particular the absolute prohibition of torture). These norms
rank above any conjunctural procedural legislation or judicial practice that subordinates
human dignity to fiscal barriers.
The interpretation and application of Article 7(3) of the Code of Criminal Procedure of
the Republic of Moldova and of Article 12¹ / Article 14 of the Code of Civil Procedure of
the Republic of Moldova in a manner that permits refusal of stamp-duty exemption to a
victim of torture who is a person with Group I disability, while simultaneously shielding
the falsification of apostilles, produces an unconstitutional judicial practice that nullifies
the essence of the constitutional order.
It is recalled that the Constitutional Court, in its Judgment of 26 September 2024,
already declared unconstitutional the absolute prohibition on exemption, deferral or
instalment payment of the stamp duty, precisely because such a prohibition
disproportionately restricted access to justice. The continued restrictive application of
the remaining provisions of Law No. 213/2023 and of the Code of Civil Procedure to
victims of torture and persons with severe disability perpetuates the same constitutional
defect.
V. Requests
For the reasons set out above, the Applicant respectfully requests the Constitutional
Court of the Republic of Moldova:
1. To declare unconstitutional the contested provisions of the State Fee Law No.
213/2023 and of the corresponding provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure of the
Republic of Moldova in the part identified in Section I above.
2. To direct the courts, when examining requests for exemption from stamp duty, to
treat the status of victim of torture and person with Group I disability as an
autonomous and sufficient ground for full exemption from payment of the fee, in
accordance with Article 4 of the Constitution of the Republic of Moldova and the norms
of jus cogens.
3. To stay the proceedings in Case No. 2r-1026/26 pending delivery of the
Constitutional Court’s judgment, pursuant to Article 12¹ of the Code of Civil Procedure.
4. To hold that the violation arising from the falsification of apostilles and from the
creation of procedural obstacles that block effective access to justice for victims of
torture is systemic in character.
