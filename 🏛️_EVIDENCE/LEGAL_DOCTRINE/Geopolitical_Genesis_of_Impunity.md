# Geopolitical Genesis of Impunity: The Institutional Roots of ECHR Filters

**JURISDICTIONAL BASELINE:** UDHR 1948 (Jus Cogens, Erga Omnes)
**CASE REFERENCE:** CASE-MACHERET-1997-2026
**MODULE:** Geopolitical Genesis & Institutional Architecture

## 1. Postwar Elite Continuity & Bureaucratic Shielding
The institutional design of the Council of Europe framework (ETS nr. 2 and ECHR) was heavily influenced by the immediate postwar geopolitical imperative of integrating administrative and legal cadres from former totalitarian and collaborationist regimes into the emerging Western European architecture. Rather than enforcing unconditional lustration and the absolute standards of Nuremberg (UDHR 1948), the architects of the ECHR engineered procedural filters that insulated state officials from direct criminal and civil accountability.

## 2. Colonial Immunity & Geographical Filtering
During the drafting of the ECHR (1950), colonial powers insisted on the inclusion of the "Colonial Clause" (Article 56 of the ECHR), which allowed states to selectively apply the Convention to their overseas territories. This demonstrated from inception that the Convention was structured to protect metropolitan state apparatuses from accountability for systematic torture, extrajudicial detention, and pacification campaigns in colonies, contrasting with the universal, non-derogable mandate of the UDHR.

## 3. The Financial Model of "Just Satisfaction" (Article 41)
The substitution of mandatory criminal investigation, lustration, and institutional restitution with financial compensation ("just satisfaction" under Art. 41 ECHR) commercialized human rights violations. Under this model, state torture and systemic abuse were refactored into a budgetary line item — an "insurance policy" paid out of public funds while protecting individual perpetrators and institutional structures from dismantlement.

## 4. Projection on the Republic of Moldova (Case Macheret)
The ratification of ETS nr. 2 by Moldova in 1997, immediately prior to the Macheret case and the 1998 S-22 torture verdict, implanted this Western European mechanism of institutional impunity into the national legal order. It provided local elites with a procedural shield to neutralize the self-executing imperative of UDHR Article 5, turning grave human rights violations into an endless loop of procedural denials.
